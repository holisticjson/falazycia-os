### 1. Guiding Principles
*   "There are no rules." (Open-mindedness in business)

### 2. Startup & Growth Framework (Three Ways to Grow)
To grow a company, focus on these three things:
1.  **Get more customers.**
2.  **Increase their average purchase value.**
3.  **Get them to buy more times.**

*(Simplified to two core ways: Get more customers, and increase each customer’s value (which encompasses average purchase value and purchase frequency).)*

### 3. Business Term Definitions
*   **Gross Profit:** Revenue minus the direct cost of servicing an ADDITIONAL customer.
*   **Lifetime Value (LTV):** Gross profit accrued over the entire lifetime of a customer.
    *   `Revenue: (Monthly Price * Gross Margin %) * Number of Months Customer Stays = Lifetime Value`

### 4. Value-Driven vs. Price-Driven Purchases
*   **Commoditized = Price-Driven Purchases** (race to the bottom).
*   **Differentiated = Value-Driven Purchases** (sell in a category of one with no comparison).

### 5. Grand Slam Offer Components
A Grand Slam Offer combines:
1.  An **attractive promotion**.
2.  An **unmatchable value proposition**.
3.  A **premium price**.
4.  An **unbeatable guarantee**.
5.  A **money model** (payment terms) that allows you to get paid to get new customers, removing cash constraint on growth.

**Impact of a Grand Slam Offer:**
*   Increased Response Rates (clicks)
*   Increased Conversion (sales)
*   Premium Prices (charging a lot of money)

### 6. Market Selection Framework ("Starving Crowd")
To identify a good market, look for four indicators:
1.  **Massive Pain:** Prospects desperately *need* what you're offering. The degree of pain is proportional to the price you can charge.
    *   **"The pain is the pitch."** Articulate the prospect's pain accurately.
    *   **Pro Tip:** The point of good persuasion is for the prospect to *feel understood*.
2.  **Purchasing Power:** Your audience must be able to afford your service at your required prices.
3.  **Easy to Target:** You can easily find and market to your ideal customers (e.g., through associations, mailing lists, social media groups, specific channels they watch).
4.  **Growing:** A growing market acts as a tailwind, making efforts easier. Avoid shrinking markets.

**Three Main Markets (Always Exist):**
*   **Health**
*   **Wealth**
*   **Relationships**

Find a smaller, growing subgroup within these buckets that meets the other criteria.

**Order of Importance (Three Levers on Success):**
`Starving Crowd (Market) > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills`

*   A "great" rating on a higher-order piece overpowers lower ones.
*   A "normal" rating pushes importance to the next element.
*   A "bad" rating stops the equation unless a "great" from a higher priority component nullifies it.

**Commit to the Niche:**
*   Niching down leads to more profit.
*   You can charge significantly more for the exact same product by niching down and making it specific to an avatar.
    *   Example: "Time Management" ($19) -> "Time Management For Sales Professionals" ($99) -> "Time Management For Outbound B2B Sales Reps" ($499) -> "Time Management For B2B Outbound Power Tools & Gardening Sales Reps" ($1997).

### 7. Pricing Principle: Charge What It's Worth (Premium Pricing)
*   **Goal:** Charge as much as humanly possible, far more than it costs to fulfill, while still providing immense value (a "steal" for the prospect).
*   **Price to Value Discrepancy:** People buy when they believe the **VALUE** they are getting is worth more than the **PRICE** they are paying.
*   **Avoid Price Competition:** Unless you can decrease costs by 90%+, do not compete on price.
*   **Virtuous Cycle of Price:**
    *   **When you decrease your price:**
        *   Decrease clients’ emotional investment.
        *   Decrease clients’ perceived value.
        *   Decrease clients’ results.
        *   Attract the worst clients.
        *   Destroy margins, limiting ability to provide exceptional experiences or scale.
    *   **When you raise your price:**
        *   Increase clients’ emotional investment.
        *   Increase clients’ perceived value.
        *   Increase clients’ results.
        *   Attract the best clients (easiest to satisfy, cost less to fulfill).
        *   Multiply margins, allowing reinvestment into systems, talent, and customer experience.
*   **Higher Price Means Higher Value (Perception):** Consumers often perceive higher-priced items as having higher value.
    *   Aim to be so much higher in price that consumers think: "This is so much more expensive, there must be something entirely different going on here." This creates a **category of one**.
*   **Customer Investment:** For services requiring customer action to achieve results, higher prices increase customer investment and adherence, leading to better results.

### 8. Value Offer Framework: The Value Equation
`VALUE = (Dream Outcome * Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) / (Perceived Time Delay * Perceived Effort & Sacrifice)`

**Goals for each driver:**
1.  **(Yay) Dream Outcome:** **Increase** (The desired end result the prospect envisions).
    *   Powerful drivers related to status: "To be perceived as beautiful," "To be respected," "To be perceived as powerful," "To be loved," "To increase their status."
    *   **Pro Tip:** Frame benefits in terms of **status gained from the viewpoint of others**.
2.  **(Yay) Perceived Likelihood of Achievement:** **Increase** (How likely the prospect believes they will achieve the result).
    *   People pay for certainty.
3.  **(Boo) Perceived Time Delay:** **Decrease** (Time between purchase and receiving the promised benefit).
    *   **Two elements:** Long-term outcome (what they buy) and short-term experience (what makes them stay).
    *   **Pro Tip: Fast Beats Free.** People will pay for speed. Focus on demonstrating fast wins to reinforce purchase decisions.
4.  **(Boo) Perceived Effort & Sacrifice:** **Decrease** (Ancillary costs, tangible and intangible, required from the prospect).
    *   Decreasing perceived effort and sacrifice massively boosts offer appeal.
    *   "Done For You (DFY)" services are generally more expensive than "Do It Yourself (DIY)" due to reduced effort/sacrifice and increased perceived likelihood of achievement.

**Perception is Reality:** It's not just about changing the actual metrics, but how the prospect *perceives* these changes.
**Psychological Solutions:** Often, psychological solutions (e.g., a map for wait times) are more effective and cheaper than logical solutions (e.g., faster trains).

### 9. Grand Slam Offer Creation (Part I: Problems & Solutions)

**Step-by-Step Process:**

**Step #1: Identify Dream Outcome**
*   Define what the customer truly wants to experience, their ultimate destination, not just the product/service itself.
    *   Example: "Lose 20lbs in 6 weeks" (for a weight loss program).

**Step #2: List Problems (Obstacles)**
*   List all struggles and limiting thoughts prospects have *before*, *during*, and *after* interacting with your product/service.
*   Think in insane detail, in sequence of the customer's journey.
*   Categorize problems according to the **four Value Equation drivers**:
    *   **Dream Outcome:** "This will not be financially worth it."
    *   **Likelihood of Achievement:** "It won't work for me specifically," "I won't be able to stick with it," "External factors will get in my way."
    *   **Effort & Sacrifice:** "This will be too hard, confusing," "I won't like it," "I will suck at it."
    *   **Time Delay:** "This will take too much time to do," "I am too busy to do this," "It will take too long to work," "It won't be convenient for me."
*   **Crucial:** You must aim to resolve *every perceived obstacle* a buyer believes they will have. A single unresolved issue can prevent a sale.

**Step #3: Solutions List**
*   Transform each identified problem into a solution.
*   Think: "What would I need to show someone to solve this problem?"
*   Use "how to" language, reversing the problem statement.
    *   Example Problem: "Buying healthy food is hard, confusing, and I won’t like it."
    *   Solution: "How to make buying healthy food easy and enjoyable, so that anyone can do it (especially busy moms!)."

### 10. Grand Slam Offer Creation (Part II: Trim & Stack)

**Sales to Fulfillment Continuum:**
*   **Easy to Sell / Hard to Fulfill:** Do as much as possible for the client (high time investment).
*   **Hard to Sell / Easy to Fulfill:** Do less for the client (low time investment).
*   **Goal:** Find a sweet spot where the offer is very easy to sell and also easy to fulfill.

**Strategy for New Grand Slam Offers:**
*   **Over-deliver like crazy initially.** Aim for clients to think, "I get all this, for only that?"
*   **"Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction."** Generate demand first, then optimize for efficiency once cash flow is established.

**Step #4: Create Your Solutions Delivery Vehicles ("The How")**
*   Brainstorm all possible ways to solve each problem identified in Step #3. Use divergent thinking.
*   Push your limits; even if you're not willing to do it now, list it.
*   **Product Delivery Cheat Codes (How to think creatively about delivery):**
    1.  **Level of personal attention:** One-on-one, small group, one-to-many.
    2.  **Level of effort expected from them:**
        *   **DIY (Do It Yourself):** Figure it out on their own.
        *   **DWY (Do It With You):** You teach them how to do it.
        *   **DFY (Done For You):** You do it for them.
    3.  **Delivery environment/medium (if live):** In-person, phone support, email support, text support, Zoom support, chat support.
    4.  **Consumption format (if recording):** Audio, video, written.
    5.  **Reply speed (for support):** 24/7, 9-5, within 5 minutes, within an hour, within 24 hours.
    6.  **"10x to 1/10th Test":**
        *   If customers paid 10x your price, what would you provide?
        *   If customers paid 1/10th your price and you had to make it more valuable, how would you do it?
        *   (This stretches your mind to find widely different solutions).

**Step #5a: Trim & Stack**
*   Review your list of solutions and their delivery vehicles.
*   **Remove:**
    *   High cost, low value items.
    *   Low cost, low value items.
*   **Prioritize (based on Value Equation):**
    *   **Low cost, high value** items.
    *   **High cost, high value** items (save these for big value adds, potentially only for 1-on-1 or very premium services).
*   **Focus on "one-to-many" solutions:** These often have the biggest discrepancy between cost (to you) and value (to the customer), especially after initial creation (e.g., software, templates, guides).

**Step #5b: Bundle into the Ultimate High-Value Deliverable**
*   Combine the remaining high-value, low-cost solutions into a compelling offer.
*   Present the solution in a problem-solution-bundle format, e.g., "Problem -> Solution Wording -> Sexier Name for Bundle."
*   Quantify the perceived value of each bundle.

### 11. Enhancing the Offer: Scarcity, Urgency, Bonuses, Guarantees, and Naming

**Fundamental Principle:** All marketing influences the supply and demand curve.
*   Increase demand (through persuasion).
*   Decrease supply (or perceived supply) to sell for more money.
*   **Perfect Profit Combination:** High demand, very little supply (or perceived supply).

**"Hormozi Law":** The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make.

### 12. Enhancing the Offer: Scarcity
*   **Definition:** Fixed supply or quantity of products/services available. Creates "fear of missing out" (FOMO) and increases the need to take action.
*   **Impact:** Unlocks unlimited pricing power.
*   **Key Principle:** People want what they can't have. Decrease or delay satisfying desires. Sell fewer units than you otherwise can.
*   **Rule:** Always sell out to build social proof and increase desire for future offers.
*   **Types of Scarcity:**
    1.  **Limited Supply of Seats/Slots:** Limit the total number of clients or availability over a period.
        *   Example: "My agency only will service twenty-five customers total. Period." Create a waiting list.
    2.  **Limited Supply of Bonuses:** Offer bonuses that are only available for a limited quantity.
    3.  **Never Available Again:** A specific product/service/edition will never be offered again.
*   **Ethical Scarcity (Honest Scarcity):**
    *   Define your actual capacity (e.g., "I can only handle 10 new clients").
    *   Publicly share your limits (e.g., "We are three-fourths to capacity this week"). This pushes prospects to act.

**Pro Tips for Scarcity:**
*   **Extreme Scarcity:** Offer very limited 1-on-1 access at a very high price. Cap it at a tiny number.
*   **"Once You’re Out, You Can Never Come Back":** Cap service levels and state that if clients leave, they cannot return. Works best with small groups.

### 13. Enhancing the Offer: Urgency
*   **Definition:** A function of time. Limit *when* people can sign up, rather than *how many*. Creates a defined deadline.
*   **Impact:** Drives decisions by decreasing the action threshold.
*   **Types of Urgency (Ethical & Consistent):**
    1.  **Cohort-Based Rolling Urgency:** Have clients start on a regular cadence (e.g., weekly, bi-weekly, quarterly).
        *   Example: "Sign up today to get in with our next group that kicks off on Monday, otherwise you’ll have to wait until our next kickoff date."
        *   Can offer a "speedy personalized onboarding" as a bonus for signing up early.
    2.  **Rolling Seasonal Urgency:** Use actual sign-up date countdowns with seasonal names.
        *   Example: "New Year Promotion ends Jan 30!" "Valentines Lovers Promo Ends Feb 30!"
        *   These must be real deadlines to maintain credibility.
    3.  **Pricing or Bonus-Based Urgency:** Make the promotion/pricing structure time-limited.
        *   Example: "Take advantage of the discount *today*." "If you buy *now*, I will add XYZ bonus."
        *   Focus on the promotion/bonus deadline, not necessarily the core service availability.
    4.  **Exploding Opportunity:** Emphasize arbitrage opportunities or competitive situations that decay over time.
        *   Example: "Sooner someone acts the better it will be for them." Job offers with decreasing bonuses for delay.

**Pro Tips for Urgency:**
*   **Clean Your Pipeline with Every Price Change:** Announce price increases with a deadline ("The price is going up! Get in now!") to drive sales from fence-sitters.

### 14. Enhancing the Offer: Bonuses
*   **Core Principle:** A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into its component parts and stacked as **bonuses**.
*   **Why it works:** Increases the prospect's price-to-value discrepancy. Establishes the core offer's price, then adds value, snapping the "rubber band" in their mind.
*   **Pro Tip:** **Add Bonuses Instead of Discounting** (whenever possible on core offers). Discounting teaches negotiation; bonuses show strength and goodwill.

**Presenting Bonuses (1-on-1 vs. Group Selling):**
*   **1-on-1 Selling:**
    *   **First Ask:** Ask for the sale *before* offering bonuses.
    *   **If YES:** Reveal additional bonuses (wow experience).
    *   **If NO:** Present a bonus that matches their perceived obstacle, then ask again. (People have a hard time rejecting reciprocity).
*   **Bonus Bullets (What to include when describing each bonus):**
    1.  Always offer them (use your bundled list).
    2.  Give them a **special name with a benefit in the title**.
    3.  **Tell them:**
        a.  How it relates to their issue.
        b.  What it is.
        c.  How you discovered it/created it.
        d.  How it specifically improves their lives or experience (faster, easier, less effort/sacrifice - connecting to the **Value Equation**).
    4.  **Provide some proof** (stat, past client, personal experience).
    5.  **Paint a vivid mental image** of their life with the bonus benefits.
    6.  **Always ascribe a price tag** and justify it.
    7.  **Prioritize Tools & Checklists** over additional trainings (lower effort/time for higher value).
    8.  Each bonus should address a **specific concern/obstacle** in the prospect's mind or solve their *next* problem.
    9.  The value of the bonuses should **eclipse the value of the core offer** (psychological bias making the core offer seem more valuable).
    10. Further enhance bonuses with **scarcity and urgency**.

**Advanced Level Bonuses (Other People's Products & Services):**
*   Get other businesses to provide their services/products as bonuses in exchange for exposure to your clients (free marketing for them, high-value at no cost for you).
*   Negotiate **group discounts and a commission** for yourself to create additional revenue streams.

### 15. Enhancing the Offer: Guarantees
*   **Core Principle:** The single greatest objection is **risk**. Reversing risk makes any offer more attractive.
*   **Impact:** Can increase conversions 2-4x.
*   **Rule:** Always hit your guarantee hard. Say it boldly and give a reason why.
*   **Math over Emotion:** Understand that increased sales from a strong guarantee usually outweigh increased refunds (e.g., 23% net sales increase even with doubled refunds).

**Types of Guarantees:**

1.  **Unconditional Guarantees:** (Strongest)
    *   **Concept:** A trial period; pay first, then decide if they like it. No questions asked.
    *   **Client Gets:** Full refund, partial refund, refund of ad spend + ancillary costs, or even return money + an additional amount.
    *   **My Take:** Very risky for high-ticket services with high fulfillment costs. Better for lower-ticket situations. Requires high confidence in product.

2.  **Conditional Guarantees:** (Most Creative)
    *   **Concept:** "Terms and conditions" apply. If X result isn't achieved in Y time, then Z (e.g., money back). Conditions should be key actions for client success.
    *   **Impact:** Powerful for getting client results by linking guarantee to necessary actions.
    *   **Sub-Types:**
        *   **Outsized Refund Guarantee:** Double or triple money back, or a significant no-strings-attached payment (requires client consumption/action).
        *   **Service Guarantee:** You keep working for them free of charge until X is achieved (eliminates time element, focuses on outcome).
        *   **Modified Service Guarantee:** You give them another Y-long period of service/access free of charge (finite extension).
        *   **Credit-based Guarantee:** Refund as credit toward any service you offer (best for upsells where clients already like existing service).
        *   **Personal Service Guarantee:** You work with them one-on-one, free, until X objective/result (very strong, requires strict conditions).
        *   **Hotel + Airfare Perks Guarantee:** Reimburse product + hotel/airfare if no value received (for workshops/in-person experiences).
        *   **Wage-Payment Guarantee:** Pay their hourly rate if your call/session isn't valuable (ancillary cost guarantee).
        *   **Release of Service Guarantee:** Let them out of their contract free of charge (voids commitment/cancellation fees).
        *   **Delayed Second Payment Guarantee:** Don't bill again until after their first outcome (e.g., lose first five pounds, make first sale).
        *   **First Outcome Guarantee:** Continue to pay their ancillary costs (ad spend, hotel) until they reach their first outcome.

3.  **Anti-Guarantees:**
    *   **Concept:** Explicitly state "all sales are final." Must be owned with a creative "reason why" (e.g., product is exclusive, powerful, or easily copied).
    *   **Impact:** Creates perceived exclusivity and attention. Works well for high-ticket products/services requiring customization or self-starters.

4.  **Implied Guarantees (Performance Models):**
    *   **Concept:** Performance-based offers where payment is tied directly to results.
    *   **Examples:** Revshare (X% of revenue), Profit-share (X% of profit), Ratchets (payment increases based on thresholds), Bonuses/Triggers (payment when Y occurs).
    *   **Impact:** Makes you accountable for client results, weeds out low performers, aligns interests, fosters long-term relationships.
    *   **Consideration:** Tracking and collection can be drawbacks. Can be paired with a minimum fee.

**Create Your Own Winning Guarantee:**
*   Identify client's biggest fears, pain, and perceived obstacles.
*   Reverse their fears into a guarantee (e.g., "What do they *not* want to have happen if they pay you?").
*   The more specific and creative, the better.
*   **My Advice:** Start with service-based guarantees or performance partnerships to ensure commitment to customer results.

### 16. Enhancing the Offer: Naming
*   **Purpose:** Attract ideal prospects, differentiate the offer, and increase interest/action.
*   **Problem:** Offers fatigue over time, especially in local markets.
*   **Solution:** Change the "wrapping paper" – rename the offer to refresh it, *without changing the actual product/service*.

**M-A-G-I-C Headline Formula (for naming programs/services):**
*   **M - Magnet (Reason Why):** Tell people the "reason why" for the promotion.
    *   Examples: Free, 88% off, Giveaway, Grand Opening, New Management, Anniversary, New Year.
*   **A - Avatar (Announce the Avatar):** Call out your ideal client specifically (who it's for, who it's not for).
    *   Be as specific as possible (e.g., "Brooklyn Busy Executives" not "Chicago").
    *   Examples: Bee Cave Dentists, Rolling Hills Moms, Brick & Mortar Businesses, Salon Owners, Retired Athletes.
*   **G - Goal (Give Them a Goal):** Articulate the prospect's dream outcome.
    *   Specific and tangible is better.
    *   Examples: Pain Free, Celebrity Smile, 1st Place, Never Out Of Breath, Perfect Product, Double Your Profit, First Client.
*   **I - Interval (Indicate a Time Interval):** Let people know the duration to expect for results.
    *   Use with caution if platforms have compliance rules against quantifiable claims with duration.
    *   Examples: AA Minutes, BB Hours, CC Days, DD Weeks, Z Months, "4 Hour," "21 Day," "6 Week."
*   **C - Container (Complete with a Container Word):** Denotes the offer is a bundle, a system, something not commoditized.
    *   Examples: Challenge, Blueprint, Bootcamp, Intensive, Incubator, Masterclass, Program, Detox, Experience, Summit, Accelerator, Fast Track, Shortcut, Sprint, Launch, System, Getaway, Transformation, Workshop, Solution, Hack, Cheatcode.

**Tips for Naming:**
*   Not all components are mandatory; typically use three to five. Shorter and punchier names are generally better.
*   Test different names to see what works best.
*   **Pro Tip: Find Time To Rhyme** (e.g., Six-Pack Fast Track, Marriage Thrive Deep Dive).
*   **Pro Tip: Alliteration** (e.g., Make Money Masterclass, Big Booty Bootcamp).
*   **Pro Tip: Name Sub Items & Bonuses:** Apply the M-A-G-I-C formula to individual components of your offer stack to enhance their perceived value.

**What Happens When Offers Fatigue (Order of Change):**
To keep lead flow consistent:
1.  Change the **creative** (images, videos).
2.  Change the **body copy** in ads.
3.  Change the **headline** (the "wrapper" of your offer).
4.  Change the **duration** of your offer.
5.  Change the **enhancer** (free/discount component).
6.  Change the **monetization structure** (series of offers, price points).

*Note: Changes lower on the list are more operationally heavy. Exhaust lighter changes first.*

### 17. Your First $100,000 (Execution Summary)
**Key Learnings & Action Points:**
1.  **Do not be a commodity.** Differentiate your offer.
2.  **Pick a normal or growing market.** Niches lead to riches.
3.  **Charge a lot of money (premium pricing).**
4.  **Charge a lot of money using the four core Value Equation drivers.**
5.  **Create your value offer in five steps:**
    *   Identify Dream Outcome.
    *   List Problems.
    *   List Solutions.
    *   Create Solutions Delivery Vehicles ("The How").
    *   Trim & Stack (High value, low cost to you).
6.  **Stack the value, deliver it, and make it profitable.**
7.  **Shift the demand curve in your favor using scarcity.**
8.  **Use urgency to decrease the action threshold of buyers.**
9.  **Strategically use bonuses to increase the demand of your offer.**
10. **Completely reverse buyer risk with a creative guarantee.**
11. **Name it in a way that resonates with your avatar.**
